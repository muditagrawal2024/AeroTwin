Amazon S3, simulated. A drone keeps the buckets busy.

AeroTwin is a working model of Amazon S3: buckets, objects, versioning, storage classes, lifecycle rules, bucket policies, presigned URLs, event notifications and billing. Its data comes from a real-time drone simulator you fly with the keyboard, a gamepad or block missions, and every flight is uploaded as S3 objects.

What the S3 simulation covers

  • Buckets and objects with real naming rules, prefixes as folders, MD5 ETags and multipart uploads.
  • Versioning with delete markers and one-click restore of any earlier version.
  • Storage classes and lifecycle rules from Standard to Deep Archive, plus a clock you can move 90 days ahead.
  • Security: Block Public Access, bucket policies with an evaluator, SSE-S3 encryption and SigV4 presigned URLs.
  • Event notifications that trigger a flight-indexer function, an AWS CLI emulator and a CloudTrail-style activity log.
  • Billing: GB-month and request metering, a budget alert, a forecast and a fleet cost calculator.
How it maps to S3

The enhancement: a drone that produces data

  • Flight logs: telemetry recorded at 10 Hz and uploaded to aerotwin-flight-logs on every landing or crash.
  • Camera: photos and video from the gimbal camera go to aerotwin-drone-media.
  • Missions are Blockly programs saved to the versioned aerotwin-missions bucket.
  • Replays read flight logs back from S3; archived logs have to be restored from Glacier first.
  • Three ways to fly: keyboard (Mode 2), any gamepad, or block missions with loops and sensors.

Keyboard

The two hands mirror the two sticks of a real transmitter: W A S D for throttle and yaw, the arrow keys for pitch and roll.

Gamepad

Any Xbox, PlayStation or generic controller works the moment you press a button, with adjustable dead zone and expo.

Block missions

Write autonomous flights with Blockly, store them in S3 with versioning, and grab the sticks at any time to take back control.
